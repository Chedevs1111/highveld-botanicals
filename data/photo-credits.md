## Rejected on the first pass

`data/rejected-photos/` holds five images that were downloaded and then discarded.
They are kept so nobody re-picks them from the same search results.

| File | Why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| `full-spectrum-cbd-oil-1000mg.jpg` | **PACHAMAMA** branding clearly legible, and the label reads 750 mg against our stated 1000 mg |
| `dark-cacao-bar.jpg` | **SATORI** branding plus a California regulatory mark; also chocolate-covered bites, not a scored bar |
| `blue-dream-live-resin-cart.jpg` | **CBDfx** branding; also disposable pens, not a 510-thread cartridge |
| `recovery-balm.jpg` | Label reads "Pain Relief CBD Salve" — a medical claim, contradicting the site's own "nothing here is medical advice" |
| `citrus-sunrise-gummies.jpg` | Rainbow gummy bears — child-appealing shapes are a standing compliance problem for cannabis edibles |

The first three are the trademark risk worth understanding: using a photograph of another
company's product to sell yours is both misleading to the customer and a trademark problem
for you, regardless of how the photo itself is licensed.

## Remaining weaknesses in the current set

- **Citrus Sunrise Gummies** is candied fruit cubes, not gummies. Unbranded and not
  child-shaped, so it is safe, but it is not the product.
- **Dark Cacao Bar** is a plain chocolate bar with no cannabis context and no visible
  10-square scoring, which the product copy makes a dosing instruction.
- **Durban Poison, Swazi Gold, Malawi Cobra** come from one shoot and share a visual style.
  Fine as placeholders, obvious as stock once you look.
- Every flower image shows **someone else's flower** while the page states a batch ID and a
  lab-verified THC percentage. That pairing is the real problem, and no stock photo fixes it.

## What to shoot instead

Per batch, on a plain mid-grey or black background, square crop:

1. Whole product in its packaging, front-on
2. Macro of the flower or product surface — this is the shot that sells cured flower
3. Scale reference (the jar in a hand, the bar with the scoring visible)
4. The COA card or batch label, legible

Consistent lighting across all products matters more than any individual shot. The card grid
puts ten of them side by side and mismatched colour temperature is what makes a store look
amateur.
